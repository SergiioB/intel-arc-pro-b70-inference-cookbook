## Dispatch attribution (the phase's entry question)

Trace: 3,338 device events = **2,876 kernels + 462 memcpys**, 217.6 ms of device time across the
window (1 prefill of 1,761 tokens + the decode steps of a 32-token request).

| family | calls | total ms | share of device time | median µs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MoE cutlass gemm (`MoE::GemmCuteName<…>`) | 46 | 90.68 | **41.7 %** | 1,987.6 |
| `gemm_kernel` | 159 | 68.14 | **31.3 %** | 309.9 |
| Mamba/SSD family (`_chunk_scan_fwd`, `_chunk_state_fwd`, `_state_passing_fwd`, `_bmm_chunk_fwd`, `_causal_conv1d_fwd`) | 115 | 17.4 | 8.0 % | 55–357 |
| act / gather / remap / norm / reductions / copies (the rest) | ~2,550 | ~41 | ~19 % | 1–193 |

**GEMMs are 73 % of device time.** The Mamba/SSD block family the phase proposes to fuse is
**8 %**, and its calls are already large (median 55–357 µs, i.e. *not* tiny fragmented launches).

**Consequence for P2's premise.** The phase's argument is that 736 dispatches/token of fragmented
Mamba work is the loss class fusion removes, worth 1.01 ms/token (≈19 % of a 5.36 ms token). This
trace does not support that: with XPU graphs on, the fragmented launches are already cheap, and the
fusable family is 8 % of device time — so **fusion cannot deliver the 19 % the phase budgets for**.
The phase's own stop rule applies: *"Stop when exposed overhead cannot support the predeclared
useful gain."*

**Caveats, stated so they are not hidden:** the window mixes prefill and decode (a decode-only
window would sharpen the split); the "736 launches/token" figure itself was not directly counted
(the window's step count is ambiguous from a streaming client); and the MoE share is inflated by
prefill's large batches. None of those caveats changes the ordering — GEMM ≫ fusable Mamba family —
which is the fact the entry decision needs.
